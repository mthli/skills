# Backtested outcomes: full findings

Two tests. The six-year replay (2021-01 → 2026-10) is the verdict; the live-history backtest (2026-05-14 → 2026-07-29) below it is in-sample and mostly didn't replicate.

## Six-year replay (2021-01-04 → 2026-10-06)

`scripts/replay_bases.py --eval-start 2021-01-04` (2026-10-07 run). Pool: the 2,971 US equities Yahoo listed above $500M on 2026-10-06; each day's universe is the names with market cap at the time (split-adjusted close × today's share count) above $5B and 63-session average volume above 1M, ~805 names/day. RS ratings are percentile ranks across that day's universe; the trend template, detect_base's window search, the score (scan.compute_base_score itself), the same-issuer dedup and the vol-collapse filter are rebuilt per session and pinned to scan.py session by session by `test_replay_bases.py`. ~54 picks/day, 13,960 closed episodes, 7,450 fills. Cross-checks: over the 59 evening run-days before the 2026-09-02 data lag the lists' Jaccard overlap is 0.94, base lengths agree 99%, scores have a median difference of 0 (pivots agree within 1% on 88%, the rest moved by later dividend re-adjustment); episodes starting in the live sample's own window reproduce the ledger (long bases +2.22%/trade vs +2.13%, 62% vs 60% win; everything −0.98% vs −1.20%).

**The measure.** The canonical trade (buy-stop at the latest pivot while listed and one session after, fill at max(pivot, open), 8% stop with the fill day judged by its close, exit at the 20th close), with the stop filled at min(stop, open), minus SPY from the fill session's open to the exit close; averaged within each fill session and then across sessions, with t on the session means (breakouts cluster on strong market days). With the ledger's own conventions instead (stop booked at exactly −8%, SPY from the fill day's close): everything −0.34% (t −2.4), long bases −0.40% (t −1.7).

## Live-history backtest (2026-05-14 → 2026-07-29 sample): in-sample, superseded

Verdicts from the replay in brackets: [✗] didn't replicate, [✓] held, [–] not re-tested. The ten findings below are the evidence behind SKILL.md's live-sample list. `scripts/backtest_outcomes.py` replays `state/history.csv` as the canonical trade: while a name stays on the watchlist, a buy-stop sits at the most recent pivot (fill = max(pivot, open) so gaps pay real slippage); dropout cancels the order. Sample: 426 testable episodes (142 triggered with a full +20-session window) over a flat tape (SPY forward-20d ≈ 0%, regime mostly RISK-ON/CAUTION). Findings, strongest first:

1. [✗] **Base length ≥ 20 weeks looked like the one big validated edge.** +4.9% avg at +20 sessions, 75% win rate, 14% stop-hit, 76% still above pivot at +10 (n=28 across 26 distinct tickers); as the stop-based trade that's **+4.9%/trade vs −0.8% baseline**. No ≥20-week trigger gapped more than 3% above its pivot in-sample; the gap-chase failure mode (#5) lives in the short-base names. Sub-10-week bases: 37% win, −4.6%. The O'Neil "bigger base → bigger move" claim survives contact with data; fresh bases are where the failures live.
2. [✗, reversed] **Volume dry-up was INVERTED vs doctrine in this sample.** Vol↓ ≥ 0.90 (no dry-up): +0.4% avg, 52% win, 37% stop-hit. Deep dry-up < 0.70: **−8.8%, 33% win, 61% stop-hit**. The toxic combo (base < 10wk AND Vol↓ < 0.9) ran −8.6% with 79% stop-hit (n=38). This mirrors the momentum-scan entry-volume finding: quiet volume in 2026's tape means dead, not coiled. It contradicts doctrine, so re-validate as history grows before hard-coding. **Survives the base-length control** (#10): restricted to sub-20-week bases, deep dry-up still runs −5.94%/trade, 20% win, 73% stop-hit against that cohort's −2.26% mean (n=15), so it isn't #1 arriving through a correlated column.
3. [✓] **The composite Base Score does not discriminate outcomes.** <50 / 50–59 / 60–69 bands are statistically identical; the four ≥70 episodes never even triggered. Its two biggest weights (tightness 25pts, dry-up 15pts) pointed nowhere or backwards in this sample. Treat Score as a display floor and rank by BaseWks instead.
4. [✓] **Baseline is no-edge: the watchlist as a whole did not pay.** 49% of episodes touch their pivot (median 1 session); post-trigger avg −2.6%, 45% win, **66% fall back below the pivot within the 5 sessions after the trigger day**; the stop-based trade averaged −0.8% with the stop hit 51% of the time. The literature 40-50% win rate reproduced, but without the asymmetric payoff, so selection (which base) is everything.
5. [✗] **Don't chase gap fills.** Fills > 3% above pivot averaged −11.5% at +20 sessions (n=6; small, but all real cases: earnings/crypto-beta gaps, not data artifacts). Fills within 1-3% were fine. If the open blows through the pivot by >3%, skip or wait for the retest.
6. [–] **Volume confirmation did not rescue entries.** Close-above-pivot on ≥1.5× volume, entering next open: 33% win, −6.3% avg (n=21, small). Confirmation costs next-open slippage (median 2.2% over pivot) and selected no better in this tape. Close-based entries without the volume gate ran a hair ahead of bare-touch (−0.3% vs −0.8%/trade) but pay a 1.5% median entry premium; neither changes any conclusion.
7. [✓] **RS slope's "sweet spot" is mostly base length in disguise** ⚠️ *(revised 2026-08-02, see #10)*. The raw split: 0–1%/wk: +0.3%, 57% win. **>1%/wk: −6.5%, 67% stop-hit** — a stock outrunning SPY that hard while "ranging" tends to be extended high-beta, not quiet accumulation. ≤0: 36% win. **The "prefer the calm-positive middle" conclusion that stood here is withdrawn**: on the refreshed ledger the 0–1%/wk band runs +1.54%/trade with a 95% interval clear of the baseline, but restricted to sub-20-week bases it runs −1.17% with the interval straddling it, and the four-band spread falls 4.9pt → 2.3pt. The band was collecting long bases, not selecting on RS. The ">1%/wk is extended" half has not been put through the same control.
8. [✗] **First appearance far from pivot is a fade signal.** Names entering the list ≥−3% from pivot: 70% trigger rate, breakeven trades. Entering at −10..−3%: 28% trigger, −3.4%/trade when they finally grind up. Also: triggers taking 6-10 sessions to fire went 22% win / 89% stop-hit (n=9): a base that takes that long to reach a pivot it started near arrives exhausted.

9. [–] **Cutting on the first close back below the pivot saves the junk and destroys the pocket.** Same fill, same 8% stop, but exit at the first close under the pivot instead of holding to +20 sessions (`exit_rule_table` in the backtest, refreshed through 2026-07-31: n=154 triggered with a full window, 30 of them ⭐ pocket). On the ⭐ pocket the cut takes **+4.45% → +0.86%/trade** (−3.59pt; it improved 6 trades and hurt 17); restricting the cut to sessions 1–5 still lands at +2.29%. On everything else it does the opposite: −2.26% → −0.29% (+1.97pt, 71 better / 21 worse), because it swaps the 8% stop for a string of ~1% scratches — win rate collapses to 16-23% while the average improves. The cut fires on 115 of 154 trades at a median session 2, i.e. on most trades: in a ≥20-week base a close back under the pivot within a day or two is noise, and the names that recover from it are exactly the ones that pay. Fellback@5 remains a strong *descriptor* (fell-back trades: −4.31%/trade, 22% win, 72% stop-hit; held: +5.26%, 74% win, 13% stop-hit) — it just is not an exit rule for the stratum worth trading. Same shape as momentum-scan's exit-rule test, where every price-based exit lost to doing nothing.

10. [✗ dry-up half; – BB%ile half] **Only two attributes survive holding base length constant, and both say "don't buy" rather than "buy"** *(added 2026-08-02)*. Method: stratify the 154 completed trades in `state/outcomes.csv` by every attribute `history.csv` records at the **first** listing day — the decision point, so nothing is read with hindsight — then re-run each split inside the sub-20-week cohort, which holds #1 out and asks whether the attribute still separates. Survivors: **Vol↓ < 0.70** (#2) and **BB%ile ≥ 50** (−3.83%/trade, 27% win, 68% stop-hit, n=37). The second is the weaker claim: every trade in that band came from a sub-20-week base, so the control cannot actually separate the two attributes here — read it as "the squeeze never happened", close to a restatement of the setup being immature. Casualty: RS slope's good band (#7). **Flat across their bands**, spread between best and worst where #1's is 7.5pt — `rank` **0.5pt** (the top-ranked name and the #50 name returned the same, so the ranker matters far less than the filter), Score 1.5pt (#3 again), Sig tier (🔥 −0.57% vs 📊 −0.72%, so the "loaded" glyph does not predict), width 4.8pt but non-monotonic, gap size 3.5pt. Stacking both exclusions lifts the whole ledger −0.95% → +0.06%/trade (the excluded 49 trades: −3.13%), but the ⭐ pocket barely moves (+4.45% → +3.96%, only 4 trades removed) and non-pocket trades stay negative at −1.22%: the exclusions cut losses, they do not make the rest pay. Two limits: ~40 band comparisons ran, so at 95% about 2 "significant" bands are expected from noise alone; and this is a **re-analysis of the same ledger as #1–#9**, not an out-of-sample test — it can correct what those findings failed to control for, but it cannot independently confirm any of them.

**Caveats**: one 2.5-month window, one (flat) regime; overlapping episodes are not independent samples; strata get small at the edges; current-large-cap universe adds survivorship optimism. #10 shares every one of these — it re-cuts the same trades rather than adding any. Re-run the backtest quarterly as history accumulates, above all to re-test the dry-up inversion (#2) and the score's usefulness (#3) in a trending tape, and to put #10's two survivors on data they weren't found in.
