# Backtested outcomes: full findings (2026-05-14 → 2026-07-29 sample)

Full evidence behind the nine-bullet summary in SKILL.md's "Backtested outcomes" section. `scripts/backtest_outcomes.py` replays `state/history.csv` as the canonical trade: while a name stays on the watchlist, a buy-stop sits at the most recent pivot (fill = max(pivot, open) so gaps pay real slippage); dropout cancels the order. Sample: 426 testable episodes (142 triggered with a full +20-session window) over a flat tape (SPY forward-20d ≈ 0%, regime mostly RISK-ON/CAUTION). Findings, strongest first:

1. **Base length ≥ 20 weeks is the one big validated edge.** +4.9% avg at +20 sessions, 75% win rate, 14% stop-hit, 76% still above pivot at +10 (n=28 across 26 distinct tickers); as the stop-based trade that's **+4.9%/trade vs −0.8% baseline**. No ≥20-week trigger gapped more than 3% above its pivot in-sample; the gap-chase failure mode (#5) lives in the short-base names. Sub-10-week bases: 37% win, −4.6%. The O'Neil "bigger base → bigger move" claim survives contact with data; fresh bases are where the failures live.
2. **Volume dry-up is INVERTED vs doctrine in this sample.** Vol↓ ≥ 0.90 (no dry-up): +0.4% avg, 52% win, 37% stop-hit. Deep dry-up < 0.70: **−8.8%, 33% win, 61% stop-hit**. The toxic combo (base < 10wk AND Vol↓ < 0.9) ran −8.6% with 79% stop-hit (n=38). This mirrors the momentum-scan entry-volume finding: quiet volume in 2026's tape means dead, not coiled. It contradicts doctrine, so re-validate as history grows before hard-coding.
3. **The composite Base Score does not discriminate outcomes.** <50 / 50–59 / 60–69 bands are statistically identical; the four ≥70 episodes never even triggered. Its two biggest weights (tightness 25pts, dry-up 15pts) pointed nowhere or backwards in this sample. Treat Score as a display floor and rank by BaseWks instead.
4. **Baseline is no-edge: the watchlist as a whole did not pay.** 49% of episodes touch their pivot (median 1 session); post-trigger avg −2.6%, 45% win, **66% fall back below the pivot within the 5 sessions after the trigger day**; the stop-based trade averaged −0.8% with the stop hit 51% of the time. The literature 40-50% win rate reproduced, but without the asymmetric payoff, so selection (which base) is everything.
5. **Don't chase gap fills.** Fills > 3% above pivot averaged −11.5% at +20 sessions (n=6; small, but all real cases: earnings/crypto-beta gaps, not data artifacts). Fills within 1-3% were fine. If the open blows through the pivot by >3%, skip or wait for the retest.
6. **Volume confirmation did not rescue entries.** Close-above-pivot on ≥1.5× volume, entering next open: 33% win, −6.3% avg (n=21, small). Confirmation costs next-open slippage (median 2.2% over pivot) and selected no better in this tape. Close-based entries without the volume gate ran a hair ahead of bare-touch (−0.3% vs −0.8%/trade) but pay a 1.5% median entry premium; neither changes any conclusion.
7. **RS slope has a sweet spot, not a gradient.** 0–1%/wk: +0.3%, 57% win. **>1%/wk: −6.5%, 67% stop-hit**: a stock outrunning SPY that hard while "ranging" tends to be extended high-beta, not quiet accumulation. ≤0: 36% win. Prefer the calm-positive middle.
8. **First appearance far from pivot is a fade signal.** Names entering the list ≥−3% from pivot: 70% trigger rate, breakeven trades. Entering at −10..−3%: 28% trigger, −3.4%/trade when they finally grind up. Also: triggers taking 6-10 sessions to fire went 22% win / 89% stop-hit (n=9): a base that takes that long to reach a pivot it started near arrives exhausted.

9. **Cutting on the first close back below the pivot saves the junk and destroys the pocket.** Same fill, same 8% stop, but exit at the first close under the pivot instead of holding to +20 sessions (`exit_rule_table` in the backtest, refreshed through 2026-07-31: n=154 triggered with a full window, 30 of them ⭐ pocket). On the ⭐ pocket the cut takes **+4.45% → +0.86%/trade** (−3.59pt; it improved 6 trades and hurt 17); restricting the cut to sessions 1–5 still lands at +2.29%. On everything else it does the opposite: −2.26% → −0.29% (+1.97pt, 71 better / 21 worse), because it swaps the 8% stop for a string of ~1% scratches — win rate collapses to 16-23% while the average improves. The cut fires on 115 of 154 trades at a median session 2, i.e. on most trades: in a ≥20-week base a close back under the pivot within a day or two is noise, and the names that recover from it are exactly the ones that pay. Fellback@5 remains a strong *descriptor* (fell-back trades: −4.31%/trade, 22% win, 72% stop-hit; held: +5.26%, 74% win, 13% stop-hit) — it just is not an exit rule for the stratum worth trading. Same shape as momentum-scan's exit-rule test, where every price-based exit lost to doing nothing.

**Caveats**: one 2.5-month window, one (flat) regime; overlapping episodes are not independent samples; strata get small at the edges; current-large-cap universe adds survivorship optimism. Re-run the backtest quarterly as history accumulates, above all to re-test the dry-up inversion (#2) and the score's usefulness (#3) in a trending tape.
